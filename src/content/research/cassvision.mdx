Cassvision is a handheld digital video camera made for Cass' 50th birthday weekend on Fire Island in April 2027. It takes its cue from the Fisher-Price PXL-2000 (Pixelvision): it isn't a modern camera with a filter applied afterward, but a purpose-built lo-fi appliance. Pick it up, pull the trigger, and it records.

The image is 160x120 at 15 fps, processed live and shown on a rear screen. It is never shown as square pixels: an analog-style reconstruction stage, standing in for the original's tape, composite video and CRT, smooths each line horizontally and keeps the lines distinct vertically, with soft scanlines. The look is automatic: poster banding, split tone, colour lag, sound-driven slitscan and glitch bursts, with an occasional Rutt-Etra SCAN that comes and goes on its own. Clips are written straight to microSD as power-loss-safe MP4s that import into iPhone Photos, and come off the camera over USB-C as a plain drive. No app and no WiFi.

## The case

The 3D model at the top is the printable enclosure: six parts shown exactly as exported from the CAD script, including a ring that holds a fused silica window in front of the lens to keep beach sand out. Drag to turn it (or switch off **Turntable**), drag the explode slider to see how the parts go together, pick a part to see it alone, and turn on **X-ray** to see the electronics inside. The screen runs the camera's own C image pipeline, compiled to JavaScript, on each frame of a raw clip. Turn on **Sound** to hear the clip and let it drive the look; **Scan now** shows a Rutt-Etra visit; **Colour** switches to black and white like the original.

## Electronics

The brain is a [Waveshare ESP32-P4-NANO](https://www.waveshare.com/esp32-p4-nano.htm): an ESP32-P4 with 32 MB of PSRAM, a MIPI camera interface, a hardware ISP and H.264 encoder, and a microSD slot, on a 50 mm square board. It takes an OV5647 camera and a 2.4-inch 320x240 screen, exactly twice the image in each direction.

The NANO plugs into a custom four-layer carrier board that turns a dev board into a camera: USB-C charging with power path (it keeps running while it charges), a 5 V boost from the 18650 cell in the grip, soft power with a latch so switching off closes the clip cleanly, a battery gauge, and plugs for the screen, the REC trigger and the LED. The notch at the rear clears the NANO's Ethernet jack and USB-A port.
